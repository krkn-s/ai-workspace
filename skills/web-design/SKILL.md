# Web Design

Visual and conversion guidance for any website work. Three pillars carry every page: layout, color, typography. Two layers sit on top: conversion and audience fit.

## Pillar 1: Layout

The solution is visual hierarchy. A big bold line reads first, a smaller line next, a tiny line last.

- Make the most important elements of a section the biggest and boldest.
- Turn down the volume on less important elements so they become inconspicuous.

### Buttons and CTAs

- Buttons must have high contrast with their surroundings. The main action on a page is clicking a button, so it must stand out.
- Never use ghost buttons (outline only, no solid background). People don't see them and don't click them.

## Pillar 2: Color

- Use a palette that matches the website owner's brand.
- Accessibility: hard-to-read colors reduce engagement. Aim for high contrast, even for users with visual impairments.
- The 60-30-10 rule (a principle for beginners, not a hard rule):
  - 60% dominant colors: grays, blacks, whites
  - 30% brand colors
  - 10% accent color: makes elements stand out, great for CTAs and buttons

## Pillar 3: Typography

Underrated, with a massive impact on readability and user experience.

Font family categories: serif, sans serif, handwritten, script, display, monospace.

Three text elements carry a page:

- H1: biggest, most prominent text on the page. It describes what the entire page is about.
- H2: subheadings. They divide up the rest of the page and guide the user's attention.
- P: paragraph text. Avoid very decorative fonts; they look cool but kill readability.

## Conversion

Good design without good conversion practices does not help clients. A clean, modern site that prioritizes looks over conversion can see sales plummet at launch. A design that ignores conversion is useless to the client.

Central principle: reduce friction. Make it very easy to find what users want, and make buying or signing up easy.

Three levers:

- Clarity: be clear about who the company or person is, what the user should do next, and why the user should care.
- Scannability: people scan rather than read. Lay out the page so visitors can bounce around and decide quickly.
- Motivation: being pretty is not enough. Speak to what drives the user to say yes or no.

## Design for the right audience

- Not for yourself, not for the client. What matters is the website visitors.
- Research who actually uses the site.
- Choose fonts, colors, and layout based on the target audience, not personal preference.
